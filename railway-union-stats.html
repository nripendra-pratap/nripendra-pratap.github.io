Railway Union, By the Numbers 🏏

Aug 24, 2026 · 6 min read · Updated —
CricketRailway Union CCCricket LeinsterStats

Most of what I write here happens in a terminal. This one happens on grass. Since 2020 I have played — matches for Railway Union CC in the Cricket Leinster open competitions, and this is the page where I keep the receipts: — runs, — wickets, and every season in between.

It is deliberately a living page rather than a write-up. The numbers below are lifted from my CricketStatz profile, and I refresh them as the season rolls on. Last refreshed —.

Career at a glance

Batting, season by season

The shape of this table is the story. The first three summers were about learning that club cricket in Leinster is quicker than it looks from the boundary. From 2024 onwards the average settles in the mid twenties and the scoring rate climbs, which is what happens when you stop trying to survive an innings and start trying to win one.

Strike rate is only calculated from innings where balls faced were recorded by the scorers, so the earliest seasons under-report it. Averages exclude not-outs in the usual way.

Bowling, season by season

The bowling has been the steadier half. 2022 was the workload year, 2023 the cheap one, and 2026 has been the wickets-at-a-cost trade you accept when you are bowling in the back end of innings rather than the top.

In the field

Catches, the occasional stint behind the stumps, and a suspicious number of run-outs.

Innings I still think about

    How this page stays current

    The whole thing is driven by one small file, assets/cricket-stats.js. It holds a SEASONS array with one entry per summer, and everything you see above (career totals, averages, economy, strike rates, career-best score and figures) is derived from it at render time. There is no second copy of the totals to forget to update.

    So the update ritual after a season, or a good week, is:

    // assets/cricket-stats.js

const SEASONS = [
  {
    year: 2026, matches: 18,
    bat:   { inns: 18, no: 4, runs: 372, sr: "121.56", hs: "68*", hsVs: "Ashbourne 2",
             fours: 31, sixes: 20, fifties: 1, hundreds: 0, ducks: 2 },
    bowl:  { ballsBowled: 390, maidens: 3, conceded: 387, wickets: 15,
             best: "4-45", bestVs: "DLR County 2" },
    field: { ctKeeper: 0, ctField: 4, stumpings: 0, runOuts: 2 }
  },
  // ...older seasons below
];
    1. Open the CricketStatz profile and pick the season from the Seas filter.
    2. Copy the batting and bowling summary rows into that season's object.
    3. Update CAREER_SR with the all-time strike rate (the one figure that cannot be recomputed, since balls faced are not published per season).
    4. Bump UPDATED, add anything memorable to HIGHLIGHTS, and push.

    A new season is a new object at the top of the array. Nothing else in the page needs touching.

    Same instinct as any other system I build: keep one source of truth, derive everything else, and make the update path boring enough that it actually gets done in September.

    The takeaway

    — summers, — matches, one hundred, and a Saturday habit that has survived a career change, a house move and several winters of promising I would get fit before April. The numbers are not going to trouble a selector. They are just a nice, honest record of showing up.

    📊 All figures come from CricketStatz, the official statistics platform for the Cricket Leinster open competitions.

    🏏 Thanks to everyone at Railway Union CC who has scored, umpired, captained or driven a car full of kit to Stradbally on a wet Saturday.
